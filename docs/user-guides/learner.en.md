# Learner guide (English)

## Start in one minute
Open Tracepad and choose **Course**, then the lesson. You do not need an account: you are a *guest* and your progress is saved on this device only (the badge at the top says so).

## Reading and running code
Each lesson is split into chapters (use the Contents list). Every code example has a **Run** button (or press Ctrl+Enter inside the code). The result appears under the code.
- **Stop** ends a program that is stuck, for example `while True: pass`. A program is also stopped automatically after a time limit.
- If a program asks for input (`input()`), a box appears under the code; type your answer and press Enter.
- You can change the code and run it again. Your changes are not saved to the lesson itself.
- Files your programs write stay on this device and are there next time you run.
- The first run downloads the Python engine (about 12 MB). After that it also works with no internet.

## Marking chapters as read
Press **Mark as read** at the bottom of a chapter. Chapters you finished show a tick in the Contents list.

## Practising
Under each chapter you find **Practice**: predictions, quizzes and exercises.
- **Predict, run, compare**: type what you think a program prints, then press *Run and compare*. If it differs, you see your guess next to the real output and a short reason.
- **Exercises** have tests. *Check my code* shows which tests pass. Ask for up to three **hints** (a nudge, then more direct); the solution unlocks after all hints. *Start again* resets the code. Your attempt history is kept.
- **Trace it** steps through a program one line at a time. In chapter 2 the **memory model** draws names, boxes and arrows so you can see what points at what.
- **Error help**: when your code fails, a plain explanation and a "what to try" line appear under the error.

## Review, progress and streak
**Review** gives a short daily set of things you are due to see again. **My progress** shows each chapter and an estimate for each concept (an estimate from your own answers, not a grade). Your streak counts days you practised; a missed day just says welcome back.

## Notes, bookmarks and search
Use the tools on any paragraph or code block to copy, bookmark or add a note. **Search** finds text in lessons you have opened (also offline). Shortcuts: `/` search, `?` list of shortcuts, `[` and `]` previous and next chapter. On a phone, switch on **Notes mode** to show the tools.

## An account (optional)
Choose **Create account**. Say how old you are; if you are under 13 you cannot make an account yourself, so ask a parent or your teacher; the administrator creates the account for you. If you have no email, tick "I have no email address" and choose a username (letters, numbers, dots and underscores). Your guest progress moves into your account the first time you sign in. Your work then follows you to any device.
In **Account** you can change your name, password and email, sign out other devices, turn on two-factor sign-in, download all your data, or delete your account. Deleting is permanent.
If you forget your password and have an email, use **Forgot password**. If you signed up with a username only, ask your teacher or the administrator.

## Classes and assignments
1. Sign in, open **My classes**, type the **class code** your teacher gave you (8 letters and numbers) and press **Join**. A class that is archived, closed or full will say so.
2. The class page shows announcements and your assignments: *To do*, *Submitted*, *Late*, *Missed*. **Open the practice** takes you to the chapter.
3. Do the practice items, then press **Submit**. Your latest answers are saved and scored at that moment. If you are offline you will be told to reconnect first. You can submit again only if the assignment allows more than one try.
4. Your teacher may hide the score until they return it. When they do you see the score, the points for each item, and any comment. You get a **notification** (top bar, with a number when something is new).
5. Late work follows the assignment's rule (accepted, a small loss per day, or not accepted). If you need more time, ask your teacher for an extension.
6. Hints never cost you points. Looking at the solution gives 0 for that item. Your teacher can see how long you spent and whether you pasted code; this is only a note, not a punishment.
7. You can leave a class on the **My classes** page.

## Settings
**Settings** changes the language (English or فارسی), theme (light, dark, high contrast), text size (80 to 200 %), a colour-blind-friendly palette, a dyslexia-friendly font, line spacing and reduced motion. Choices stay on your device.

## Good to know
- Persian: the whole interface is in Persian, but the lesson text is still English for now.
- Your practice marks are saved by your browser. Teachers see them as *self-reported*; only an assignment you submit is scored by the system.

## Flowcharts

Every example in a lesson has a **Flowchart** button. It draws the example as boxes and arrows: rounded boxes are the start and end, rectangles are steps, slanted boxes show or ask something, diamonds are yes/no questions, and loops have an arrow going back up.

- Turn on **Trace it** and press the step buttons: the box that is running lights up, boxes already done are tinted, and a loop shows how many rounds it has run.
- Change the code and the chart is redrawn from your code a moment later. If the code is not a complete program yet, it says so.
- Under each chart there is a text version of the same steps, for screen readers or if you prefer reading.
- On a phone the chart scrolls sideways inside its frame.
- In a prediction question the chart appears only after you answer.
- The chart words are English only for now.
