# Teacher guide (English)

You need a teacher account: ask the administrator for the **teacher invite code**, sign in, open **Account** and enter it under "Become a teacher". Then **Teach** appears in the top bar.

## Create a class
1. **Teach → Class name → Create class.** Optionally pick your organization and a term (for example "Autumn 2026").
2. The class page shows the **join code**. Give it to learners (board, message). They open **My classes**, type it and press Join. Codes are 8 letters and numbers, case does not matter.
3. **Settings** tab: make a new code (the old one stops working; choose an expiry if you like), close joining without changing the code, add a co-teacher or a teaching assistant from your organization, duplicate the class for next term (with or without assignments), archive it (read-only for learners, hidden from your list) or delete it.

## Assignments
**Assignments → New assignment.** Choose the chapter, tick the practice items to include and set points (default: prediction 1, quiz 1, exercise 5). Set when it opens (release), when it is due, optionally when it closes, what happens to late work (accept, lose a percentage per day up to a cap, or refuse), how many times a learner may submit, the weight in the overall grade and when learners see results.
- **Draft** assignments are invisible to learners. **Publish** notifies the class.
- **Extensions**: give one learner a different deadline, with a reason.
- Learners press **Submit** on the class page. At that moment the system saves their answers, scores them and records the time. They see "Submitted"; the score appears only when you return it (if you chose that).

## Read the class: Overview
- **Heat map** (by chapter or by concept): each cell is the share of practice items the learner got right; a dash means not tried. These are **self-reported by the learners' browsers**; use them to see where help is needed, not as grades.
- **Who may need help**: nothing done for a week after starting, an item missed three or more times, or not started.
- **Most missed items** with the wrong answers learners actually gave (common misconceptions), **hint use**, **score distribution**. Tick "Live updates" to refresh every 15 seconds while you watch a lesson.
- **Students**: pick one to see a timeline of their work.

## Grade: Gradebook
- One row per learner, one column per assignment, an overall weighted grade. Not handed in counts as 0 only after the deadline.
- Click a cell to **review**: every item with points earned, the code the learner had at submission (**Re-run in my browser**), how long they spent, pastes and whether a solution was revealed. These signals are **observations, not proof**: a learner may paste their own earlier code. Nothing is penalised automatically.
- **Set score by hand**: needs a reason; the history of manual scores (who, when, old, new, why) is kept and cannot be edited. Add a **comment** for the learner.
- **Return to student** (or **Return results** for the whole column) makes the score and comment visible to learners and notifies them. You can hide it again.
- **Download CSV**, **Download LMS CSV** (name, username, one column per assignment) or **Print or save as PDF**.

## Announcements, comments, notifications
**Announcements** post to the class (pin the important one). Learners and you can comment on a submission; both sides are notified. **Notifications** (top bar) lists what happened; choose which kinds you want on that page.

## Organizations
**Teach** also lists organizations. An administrator can see each class's size, assignments, submissions and average, but **not individual learners' work**. To let an administrator see more, add them to your class as co-teacher.

## Things to keep in mind
- Scores are computed by the system from what the learner's browser reported; it cannot re-run their code. Open the code when a result matters. Treat Tracepad grades as classroom grades, not high-stakes proof.
- Under-13 learners cannot create accounts themselves; the administrator creates them (see the administrator guide). Arrange consent with parents or the school.
- There is no e-mail: learners see notifications when they open Tracepad.
- Persian screens and guides were written by the builder and have not been reviewed by a native teacher.

## Flowcharts in lessons

Each example has a flowchart that students can open and follow step by step with the tracer. Good uses: project one example and step through it together; ask students to change a condition and watch the chart redraw; ask which box runs next before pressing the step button. Charts are generated from the example's code, so nothing needs to be drawn when a lesson changes. Very long examples (over 45 boxes) and classes are not charted fully; the page says so.
